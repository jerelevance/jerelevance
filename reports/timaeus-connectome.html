How the trilogy thinks

Read the three dialogues as one brain. These are the concepts it keeps firing, traced through the links that repetition has worn in, to the feeling each one lands on. Each chain is tagged by how directly the text supports it.

  1. justicethe larger letters of the cityeach part doing its own workthe soul's three parts in tune729 times the tyrant's pleasurevindication

    RepublicGlaucon asks for justice to be shown good in itself; nine books later the answer is a harmony inside the soul and a number: the just king lives "729 times more pleasantly."

  2. the caveshadows taken for thingsturning the eye of the soulthe Good, like the sunmade to descend againduty over contemplation

    RepublicBook VII: the freed prisoner sees the sun, and the founders refuse to let him stay; he owes the city that raised him and goes back down to rule.

  3. goldthe royal lie of metalsthe miscounted nuptial numberwalls of orichalcumthe divine portion diluteddread of mixture

    Republic + CritiasEach link is in the text: gold souls, the breeding number that mixes metals, Atlantis's precious walls, a god's nature thinned by "the mortal admixture." Running them as one chain is a reading across dialogues.

  4. circlemotion on the same spotthe Samethinking the same thoughts about the same thingsself-sufficiencydivine rest

    TextThe sphere turns in place because that motion is "most appropriate to mind and intelligence"; the fixed stars spin and "continue to think consistently the same thoughts"; the head is a sphere copying it. The circle fires in cosmology, astronomy, anatomy and ethics alike.

  5. disordernecessity, the errant causethe Receptacle swayingwinnowingmind persuadesrelief of measure

    TextThe maker found everything "moving in an irregular and disorderly fashion" and judged order "in every way better." Creation is measure imposed on chaos, and chaos is never fully gone.

  6. bodyinflux and effluxsensation floods the soulthe circles benta man upside down, left taken for rightreason in danger

    TextThe embodiment passage: nutriment and sensation stop the Same and twist the Other, so that "they speak of the same or of the other in a manner the very opposite of the truth." Infancy, disease and vice all run back through this link.

  7. sightthe starsday, night, months, yearsnumbertimephilosophygratitude for the greatest gift

    Text"From this source we have derived philosophy, than which no greater good ever was or will be given by the gods to mortal man." The eye's job is to copy heaven's courses into ours.

  8. proportionthe mean binds the extremesfriendship of the elementssoul matched to bodyhealth and virtuethe fair

    TextThe same word does physics and ethics: proportion makes the world "indissoluble" and gives it "the spirit of friendship," and later "everything that is good is fair, and the fair is not without proportion."

  9. appetitea wild beast chained at the mangerfar from the council chamberthe liver's frightening imagescontainment

    TextThe gods house desire below the midriff so it makes "as little noise and disturbance as possible." Even the bowels' coils exist to stop gluttony from making us "an enemy to philosophy."

  10. Egyptian record"you Hellenes are children"floods erase memorywritten in the temples"the truth of the story is a great advantage"authority from antiquity

    Text + JowettThe chain is all in the frame story. Whether Plato means it straight or is winking is Jowett's reading: he calls the Solon provenance "a literary trick."

  11. failure to use reasonreborn as a womanthen a beast shaped like its vicehead pressed to the earththe hierarchy of kinds as a moral ranking

    TextWomen from men "who were cowards or led unrighteous lives," birds from naive stargazers, quadrupeds from those who stopped using their heads, fish from the most ignorant. Biology is a punishment ledger.
